# G1 development decision — 28 September 2026

**NO_GO: learned RBC is stopped on this controlled workload.** The 500-case development screen measured 33.6% exact joint-support accepted coverage, 31.6% oracle-marginal-product coverage, and 33.6% conventional-parser coverage. All three made zero accepted mistakes on these generated cases. The 2-point joint–marginal gap falls below the frozen 10-point continuation threshold; the ordinary parser also reaches the oracle ceiling. Neither result supports spending the laptop budget on learned heads here.

The policy for each independent source case was chosen from the frozen development pool using its public workflow and queried item. The parser received public text and the declared schema. The oracle and marginal-product controls used evaluator-only posterior labels. The 500 cases include the declared explicit, relational, and missing-information mixture, with 25% plain and 75% alternative renderer weights. Twenty saved text/program pairs were inspected by the coding agent, with no apparent mismatch. The saved prediction rows and support masks can be replayed with `python -m rbc verify --run runs/poc-20260928-01`.

The G1 development screen is a rejection test, not a final risk claim. The one-sided multiplicity-adjusted accepted-risk upper bound for the parser is about 2.41%, above the project's 2% target. Its zero observed errors do not establish that target. There was no final calibration/test evaluation, no learned-model training, no independent writing set, and no local-LLM call. Representation has a positive exact XOR fixture but only a small workload-average headroom gap. Learning and auxiliary supervision remain untested. External validation was not performed.

The follow-up verification used 256 repeated, named stress fixtures. The parser matched their declared outcomes, including fail-closed contradictory and unsupported text; these are not independent risk samples. A separate numeric correlation-shift counterexample gave 128/128 wrong accepted XOR actions under frozen old calibration and zero accepted actions after separate shifted recalibration. That counterexample uses synthetic scores, not a trained RBC model. On the M3 Max, 100 warm parser cases gave a 0.131 ms median compile and a 1.028 ms median compile-plus-16-policy time; no learned or LLM route was timed. Monetary sensitivity in the report is hypothetical.

Smallest justified next experiment: draft a distinct, independently written case set and a competent relational parser before reopening learned RBC. Preserve the current negative result for the frozen generated workload.
